# T3 as built (2026-11-17)

T3 is lane T's last PR (`tasks.md` lane T; d3 §K1, "T3: the egress memory leg moves to `gateway/memory/` and keeps its own workerd config"): the egress memory leg off its hand-written probe and onto the doors' and the gateway's real bundles, and with it `GATEWAY_KEYRING` required, as T0b left it (list 6, item 6i).

## The plan (research, before building)

**What T0b left.** T0b (#5510) made the declared secrets required, but not `GATEWAY_KEYRING`: `gateway/src/keyring.ts` `KeyringEnv` kept `GATEWAY_KEYRING?: unknown`, because the owner memory probe's one multi-role Env (`owner/memory/egress-probe.ts`) bound it in its gateway role only, and a required binding failed `tsc` there. Its body: "T3 moves that leg to `gateway/memory/`, with its own config, and makes the keyring required". List 6 item 6i files it to T3. The config already requires it (`gateway/wrangler.jsonc` `secrets.required`), the generated `GatewayEnv` types it `string`, and the deploy kit takes every Worker's required secrets from its config (`deploy/facts.ts` `required`), so the kit's binding list needs no change. Every test config states it: the gateway's, the owner's, the doors' and the kit's `auxiliary` gateways, and the browser test.

**Where the leg runs today.** `owner/memory/egress.mjs`, called at the end of the owner's leg (`owner/memory/leg.mjs`), bundles `owner/memory/egress-probe.ts`: one module, three Workers by `PROBE_ROLE`, which imports the doors' `serveEgress` and the gateway's `EgressGateway`, `weighedBody`, `seal` and meter, and adds routes of its own (`/gc`, `/hoard`, `/parse`, `/pool`, `/seal`). Its `/parse` re-implements the metered path's parse, stringify and encode. It is the owner workspace's last B23 entry (`owner/guards/rules.ts` `OWED`), and the platform's last owed import.

**The decided shape** (b23 research question 5 and its T3 plan; checked against today's code first):

1. The leg is `gateway/memory/egress.mjs`, the gateway's `test:memory`, its own CI leg. Its Workers are the doors and the gateway bundled as a deploy bundles them (`ci/workers.ts` `bundle`), and `gateway/memory/leg-workers.ts`: the provider with its checkpoints, the doors' `OWNER` (a fake owner object, typed by the contract's `OwnerDoors`) and `CONTROL` (a KV service), and a tool that seals through the gateway's real `ConnectGateway` and mints the egress token. Nothing imports `doors/src`.
2. Its own workerd config, not miniflare (miniflare's outbound path holds a second copy of an upload: the research's upload rows). Each Worker's date and flags are its config's.
3. Readings from outside, through the inspector; the parse's phases by breakpoints in the bundle, each found by its statement's text and failing loudly when not there exactly once; the pool by behaviour.
4. Proof: every row the old leg checks, against the same bounds, old and new side by side once, then the old leg deleted. A finding in the gateway is lane V's, not a reason to keep the probe.

Checked against today's code, three things differ from the research's prototype, which ran in miniflare:

- **Collecting.** The prototype read a heap snapshot because miniflare's inspector proxy answered neither `HeapProfiler.collectGarbage` nor `Runtime.evaluate`. In the leg's own workerd, `collectGarbage` does not answer either, but `Runtime.evaluate("gc()")` in the context `Runtime.executionContextCreated` names does (`--expose-gc`), paused or not. So the leg reads what the old leg read, `usedSize + backingStorageSize` after a collection, a 5 ms wait and another (measured: a dropped 20 MiB buffer reads 20.66, then 0.66 MiB), and the bounds keep their meaning.
- **The host.** The doors route by origin, and Node's `fetch` sends the address it dials as the host, so the leg sends to the doors through `node:http` with the egress host and `x-forwarded-proto`, which the socket's `forwardedProtoHeader` reads (`requested`). The own-workerd guard counts `requested(` as a request.
- **The control.** The old control was a door route that read a body whole (`/hoard`), probe code in the doors. With no probe code there, the control puts 11.25 MiB in each isolate through its inspector and must read at least 9 MiB more, so the leg proves it can see each isolate it measures.

## As built

1. **`ci/own-workerd.mjs`** is a leg's own workerd: `ownWorkerd` (moved from `owner/memory/workerd.mjs`), `freePorts` (from `owner/memory/ports.mjs`, deleted), `WORKERD` (the binary, which each leg resolved by its own copy of one line) and `capnpModules` (a bundle's files as a workerd Worker's modules, which `baselines.mjs` wrote by hand). Its guard moved with it (`ci/guards/own-workerd.test.ts`), and finds the legs as every workspace's `memory/*.mjs`, not a list.
2. **The leg's cases are the old leg's,** in order, each against its old bound, through the real doors and gateway: the control; the door and the gateway at 3.75 against 15 MiB, held; the gateway uploading; 4 against 8 calls; the parse's eleven shapes, each now a model call of the metered model so the real path reaches its encode; and the pool. The old `held_slots === 16` (`/pool`) goes: the pool is read by behaviour, a call behind a whole-pool upload answering 200 after it and 503 past the pool's wait.
3. **The parse's pauses** are three breakpoints in the gateway's own script source (`Debugger.getScriptSource`, so its lines are the inspector's): `read.text = null;` and `return { text` in `serialized`, `call.text = null;` in `prepared` (`forward.ts`), each name allowing the number the bundler adds (`text3`). A pause is read by its frame's line, since `hitBreakpoints` named the last breakpoint set, not the one hit. The weight is read after the last sample, from `prepared`'s own holder through the frame's local scope (`Runtime.getProperties`), never by an evaluation in the frame: one kept the frame's values past it ("DevTools console" in the snapshot) and so held the tree into the next phase. A first call through every pause runs unsampled, so the debugger's records of the script (about 1.1 MiB, made at its first pause) are in every floor; without it the first shape read 1.1 MiB over the rest.
4. **The finding: the metered path held the tree while it encoded.** On the real bundle, at `prepared`'s encode, the gateway held the tree beside the text and its bytes: for every shape the encode read the stringify's figure plus the bytes, and three shapes passed their weight (mixed 11.08 against 8.48 MiB, escaped 10.19 against 8.48, ASCII 6.21 against 4.50). A heap snapshot at the pause traced the tree to the stack's roots: `prepared`'s frame kept what it passed to `clamped` and `JSON.stringify` after `parsed = null`, the same effect `weighedBody`'s comment records for the read. The old probe's `/parse` wrote its own stringify, with no such call, and dropped its output before its encode sample, so it could not see it. The fix is the shape `weighedBody` already has: the parse, the checks, the reserve, the clamp and the stringify are `serialized`, a function of its own, whose frame is gone when `prepared` encodes. It is lane V's code, fixed here because the leg it is found by is required and the fix is a split, with no number changed (the research's rule was for the upload rows, which read within bound).
5. **`GATEWAY_KEYRING` is required:** `KeyringEnv` is `Pick<GatewayEnv, "GATEWAY_KEYRING">`, the generated, config-required type, so no copy of the binding's type is left (`test/support.ts`'s `Env` was one, and goes). The tests that hand the keyring a missing or non-string ring (`keyring.test.ts`, `keyring-main.test.ts`) keep their cases, each env asserted to the type: what the runtime hands over is still read, and refused loudly.
6. **The last owed import goes:** the owner's `OWED` is empty, and so is the layer guard's across the platform. `ci/kit/owed.ts` `imports`, which made those entries, has no caller and goes, as does the layer guard's assertion that some violation exists (`isolation.test.ts`), which the empty list makes stale.
7. **CI:** the gateway's `test` is `test:workerd && test:memory`, as the owner's. Its `platform-tests` leg runs `test:workerd`, and `platform-checks` gains `gateway memory` beside `owner memory` (`tests.yml`, `ci/guards/legs.test.ts`).

The parse and stringify phases read about 0.3 MiB more than the probe's, the real call's own state beside its body (its request, the doors' stream, the hold), the same for every shape. The encode phase reads the text and its bytes (4.21 MiB for 2 MiB of ASCII), where the probe read the bytes alone (2.00): `held.ts` says the encode holds both, and now that is what is measured.

**Headroom.** The weights are fit to the measured multiples by design (4.25 against 4.00 a byte, 2.25 against 2.00), so the text shapes sit within 6% of their weight on main and within 2.4% here (mixed, 8.28 against 8.48). That is not the 20% rule's case: these are byte counts, the same run to run on any runner, not a time a slower runner stretches; the rule's reason, runner variance, does not apply. The 0.3 MiB is a constant a call; at the 15 MiB a non-ASCII body may be, the weight's 0.25 a byte leaves 3.75 MiB.

**Mutations**, each failing on this head:

- the tree kept through the encode (`serialized` answering it beside the text): mixed, escaped and ASCII past their weight;
- the door keeping the body whole to the call's end (`doors/src/egress.ts`): `doors, held` 11.77 MiB past a quarter chunk;
- the gateway's pool a slot larger (`GATEWAY_POOL_SLOTS` 17): the pool's rows answer 200 where 503 was due;
- the encode's statement gone from `prepared`: the leg fails at once, naming it;
- an env without the keyring (`doorbell.test.ts` `ringDoorbell({}, …)`): refused by `tsc`, where main took it;
- the leg's `ask` without `own.check()`: the own-workerd guard fails.
