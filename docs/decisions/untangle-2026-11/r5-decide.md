# R5 P-decide as built (2026-11-17)

R5's second slice (d3 §N2, §N4; `r5_research.md` §3, §5.2, §6 slice 2), on P-shape (#5531, its pin #5537). The research is the design; what follows is how it landed and where it departs.

1. **One decide function, in the owner object (`owner/src/actions.ts` `Actions.decide`).** It reads the policy at the call, takes the rules on the call's act that hold for its actor (a rule's `agent`, when it names one, is that agent's runs alone), and the strictest wins: `never` refuses (`action_never`), `ask` holds (`held_for_owner`, the card's id in the refusal's facts), and no rule allows. Only an agent's call is decided: a run's (its agent, and its client when one started it) or an outside client's (the owner's main agent among them). The owner itself, and another owner visiting a collection, are never decided: the rules are the owner's on its agents.
2. **The step is the runner's, from the row's `act`.** A door whose row names an act, at a caller an agent can be (`account`, `visitors`), runs one unit before its handler: the bind, account, guard, scope and fields steps, the row's condition (`when`), then the decision. One unit a call, not one a check, so a `units` handler's later checks spend no approval twice. An owner with no rule on the act runs no such unit: the runner reads the policy first, and only a rule on the act costs the unit. The conditions are a switch over `acts.json`'s, so a new condition is a type error until it has a case:
   - `outside_member`: the post goes to an outside agent (the ledger's recipient, by the ledger's own actor), neither the owner nor one of its own agents;
   - `public_row`: the row is written with `private` false.
3. **The session doors and the sync are not decided, by their callers.** `security.change` and `access.grant` are done only at owner-session doors (`action` on each row), so no agent reaches them: they are already hand-off on every engine (research §2.2), and a rule on them holds nothing more. The sync agent's commit (`fileSyncCommit`, caller `lease`) is a run's files landing; holding it is the later line (hold-on-sync for `paths`, tasks.md R5). Until then a `workspace.files` rule holds at the file doors, and from P-hooks at the CLIs' file tools.
4. **Cards and once-approvals are one table, `action_requests`** (migration 17). A row is a held call: its card (what the owner is shown), the card's digest (what the owner's answer names), the call's digest (what an approval admits: the door, the request as asked and the actor), the client whose share it takes, the policy generation it was asked under, when it was filed, and when the owner said yes. The research's "held-card store plus a once-approval" are one row: an approval is the card answered yes, and its one use deletes it.
   - **Bounded:** `ACTION_LIMITS.held` (200) rows in all, pending or approved, `perClient` (50) of them one outside client's (counted on its index), each card's shown request at most `shownChars` (8,000) code points.
   - **Generation-bound:** a card asked under another policy generation is neither shown, answered nor admitted, so an owner's rule change re-decides every call held under the old rules.
   - **Swept:** at each decision and each answer, rows past their life go first: a pending card at `cardTtlS` (30 days: Claude Code keeps a session that long, `cleanupPeriodDays`, which bounds P-resume's resume), an approval at `approvalTtlS` (a day: a main agent retries when its user says so), and every row of another generation.
   - The same call held again answers the same card, so an agent that retries files nothing new.
   - **The card shows the request as asked, cut to `shownChars`** (`cut` true past it): every card has one shape, whatever the call; a file's bytes show as their text. The approval binds to the call's digest, never to what was shown (the bytes by their SHA-256), so a cut card admits only the call it names. The card is at most `ACTION_CARD_BYTES` (64 KiB, a frozen bound the schema's CHECK spells): 8,000 code points at 4 bytes and the card's few fields.
   - **Not exported** (`NOT_EXPORTED`): a held call lives a month at most and is in flight, as an upload is; a restored owner's agents ask again.
   - **Counted by `COUNT(*)`, not a trigger counter:** the table holds 200 rows at most, read only when a call is held, so a counter column and its triggers would buy nothing.
5. **The decisions' receipts are a bucket of their own, in a table of their own (`action_receipts`, migration 17), kept to the policy's `receiptsPerBucket` by a count on `policy_usage` their triggers keep, and listed by `policyReceipts` beside the policy's own (`actions`).** A decision is receipted when a rule decided it: `denied` (never), `held` (a card filed), `admitted` (an approval used); the owner's answers are `approved` and `refused`. A call no rule decides writes nothing, so an owner with no rules pays no row per call; a held call asked again writes no second receipt.
   - **Departure: not a fourth bucket of `policy_receipts`.** That table is exported, and its `bucket` and `kind` CHECKs change only by a rebuild. A migration that names an exported table needs a forward form for every older generation (`schema.ts` `FORWARD`; `r-restore-schema.md` item 2), and none is built yet: the first is lane R's to build, with its frozen fixture. A null entry stays true only for a migration that touches no exported table, so the bucket is its own table, not exported (`NOT_EXPORTED`, the reason stated there).
   - **Owed:** once a forward form exists, the bucket can join the export, with its own capture triggers. Until then a restore keeps the policy's receipts and starts the decisions' afresh.
6. **The owner's doors: `actionRequest`, `actionApprove`, `actionRefuse`.** The first shows a pending card (caller `owner`, as `policyProposal`); the two answers are the owner's in its session (caller `session`, rule `action_answer`), each by the card's id and the digest it was shown, so a card changed or answered since is refused. A yes keeps the row as a once-approval for `approvalTtlS`; a no deletes it. The app reaches them at `actions/request`, `actions/approve` and `actions/refuse`. A held call adds a notice (`action_request`, naming the agent, the act and the card), so the owner app's notices list is where the owner finds it.
7. **A rule's `agent`, and the agent id's rule moved to the contract.** `AGENT_ID` and `agentId` are `contract/src/text.ts`'s (from `owner/src/authority.ts` `AGENT_DIR`), so the policy's validator holds a rule's `agent` to the one grammar the ledger and the run doors hold an agent's directory to. `ledger.ts` and `owner-do.ts` import it there.
8. **Owed.**
   - P-resume: an approval starts the conversation's resume run; the call's digest then binds to the run's conversation, which a run's account does not carry yet (`runs.ts` `GRANTS`, lane O's file). Until then an approval admits the same call by the same agent under the same authority, in any of its conversations, once, within the day.
   - The pin PR for migration 17 (`owner/migrations.json`), after merge.
   - `owner/src/inbound.ts:69` `AGENT` is a narrower copy of the agent id's grammar (lowercase, 64), held by its column's CHECK (`length(agent) BETWEEN 1 AND 64`). Making it `AGENT_ID` widens what an inbound binding names and needs the table rebuilt: inbound's own change, not this slice's.
