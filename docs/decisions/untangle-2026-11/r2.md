# R2 as built (2026-11-14)

R2 is lane R's second PR, the operator channel (d3 §G; `operator_research.md` §1.4, adopted whole). It builds the channel alone: the message, the doors' consumer, the operator bucket and its lock, the keyring, and the kit's sending side. Modes `control` and `owner` are R3's. `sweep`, restore and the off-account copy come later.

1. **No op is wired yet, a departure from the research's PR order.** Its §6 puts `ownerState` and the `owner.*` ops in the channel's PR. Of those, only `owner.state` needs a new owner door (`ownerState`: a `doors.json` row, the `OwnerDoors` method and its handler in `owner/src/admission.ts`), which is lane O's file while lane O has T1c in flight. `owner.tier`, `owner.suspend`, `owner.lift` and `owner.admit` call doors that exist (`tierSet`, `admissionSet`, `putOwner`). They move to R3 anyway, so each op's args and refusals are designed once, with mode `owner` and its plan. So `OPS` in `doors/src/operator.ts` is empty here. The channel is proved without them: the MAC, the acceptance record and the answer each made once, the replay, the redelivery and an erased owner, through the doors' own queue handler. R3 adds `owner.*` to `OPS`, with `ownerState` after lane O, and carries the research's PR 2 proof for them: tier, suspend, lift and admit against a real object, and a redelivery that changes nothing.
2. **The op is the consumer's to read, after the gates.** Gate 2 holds the message's envelope to its exact shape: `v`, `op` by grammar, `owner`, flat `args`, `ask`, `at`, `kid` and `mac`. After gate 5, an op that `OPS` lacks is answered `invalid`, and from R3 on so is an op's own args refused. The research says the args are "parsed exactly" but doesn't say at which gate. The choice follows from its own ends:
   - every write still comes after the MAC, so nothing unauthenticated is ever written;
   - the kit gets a loud answer naming the refusal, instead of a 120-second wait;
   - the audit log holds every authenticated attempt;
   - with no op wired, the channel is still proved end to end: the MAC, the acceptance record, the answer, the replay and the redelivery, through the doors' own queue handler.
3. **`args` is a flat object.** Its values are strings, safe integers and booleans, and each key is a lowercase word. Its canonical text is its JSON with the keys sorted (`argsText`). The research's `sha256(canonical args)` names no canonical form. Every op it lists takes scalar fields: a tier, a reason, a flag, a generation. A flat shape needs no general canonical JSON, of which the platform already holds four copies (`policy-document.ts`, `sandbox/template/image.ts`, `owner/src/ledger.ts`, a runner test). A later op that needs nested args takes a new `v`.
4. **No `cursor` yet.** The research adds one for a continuation. No op continues until `sweep`, which adds it with the continuation it carries.
5. **The MAC is HMAC-SHA-256 under the ring's raw key** (`ring[kid]`), as the research says, not under an HKDF-derived key. The domain string `tinyassets-operator-v1` keeps it apart from any other use, and the ring has no other use. It is computed and checked with WebCrypto, `crypto.subtle.verify` doing the constant-time compare. So the one module, `contract/src/operator-message.ts`, runs in both the doors and the kit under Node's type stripping (it imports its leaves by `.ts` path, as `control.ts` does). `envelope.ts` now exports `fromBase64url`, so nothing copies it.
6. **Accepted once, answered once.** Both the acceptance record and the answer are made by an R2 put with `onlyIf: { etagDoesNotMatch: "*" }`: a put that only makes. Miniflare models the wildcard (`includesEtag`, `type: "wildcard"`). If two deliveries race, the loser of the acceptance reads the winner's record and is held to it. The loser of the answer keeps the winner's answer.
7. **The window applies to the first acceptance only:** `|now − at| ≤ 600 s`. A message seen before goes on past it, which is what lets a redelivery finish (research gate 5). A message unseen after 10 minutes, or signed 10 minutes ahead, is dropped.
8. **The lock covers the whole bucket.** It is one Age rule of 365 days with no prefix, not separate rules on `asks/` and `answers/`, because the bucket holds nothing else. So the prefixes live only in the contract module, with no copy of them in `platform.json`.
   - The kit sets it, from `platform.json`'s `bucket_locks` (keyed by binding, so no bucket name is copied), in a new storage step after the buckets.
   - The step sets the rule when the bucket holds no rule or another, and plans it without `--yes`.
   - It uses wrangler's own API: `GET` and `PUT /accounts/{a}/r2/buckets/{b}/lock`, `{rules: [{id, enabled, prefix, condition: {type: "Age", maxAgeSeconds}}]}` (`getBucketLockRules`, `putBucketLockRules`, wrangler 4.154.0).
9. **The kit reads an answer through the REST client, not S3.** It uses `GET /accounts/{a}/r2/buckets/{b}/objects/{key}`, wrangler's `getR2Object`, under the deploy token, which already makes buckets. So the operator path needs no R2 access key. A 404 with R2's 10007 means absent, and any other failure throws, as `value` does for KV.
10. **The producer binding `OPERATOR` has no sender in the doors yet.** It is the queue's one home: `QUEUES.OPERATOR` is generated from it, the entry dispatches by it and the kit sends to it, and the kit makes the queue from it (`facts.ts` `QUEUES`). The research lists it, and the sweep's continuation will use it.
11. **`facts.ts` reads a bucket and a dead-letter queue by binding once** (`bucketOf`, `deadLettersOf`). `IMPORT_NAMES` and the new `OPERATOR_NAMES` both use them, so the operator's names copy no lookup of the import's.
