# The first nightly recovery copy

The manifest lane promises its first copy on the night after the object first serves its owner, at the owner-specific UTC offset. An ordinary owner can have no recovery copy indefinitely on `f934871ad`. The first call to `Backups.next` schedules tomorrow. At tomorrow's wake, `Backups.run` asks again, sees zero finished copies and schedules the following night. The original Worker test checks that first appointment, inserts a synthetic finished generation, then drives the alarm.

On main, a real local Worker owner wrote a folder, observed the manifest job and drove the genuine alarm at its recorded time. Zero generations finished. Using the actual 18 migrations, gate, change log, backup run and next-work calculation on Node 22 and 24, seven consecutive wakes left zero generations for both a new owner and an owner whose first attempt had been given up. The same five cases ran on main, joined `23361076e` and `cfdcc0441`. An unbound and an idle fully backed-up owner scheduled nothing. A changed owner with a finished generation opened the next one on its first due wake. The first-night issue already existed in #5403. Migration 12 in #5611 records the last actual attempt so an abandoned one waits for its next night. The computation still masks that date whenever zero generations are finished.

The backup lane owns its cadence. Its existing private `owner_backup_state.began_at` supplies the date from which its next night is counted. The first binding starts that cadence. Each actual generation opening sets its date, even if that generation is later given up. When a restore adopts a generation, its root supplies its original date. The newer of the retained generation's day and the cadence date determines the next night. Admission alone does not bind an owner. The first accepted non-admission door, including the first import call, must store the owner and the cadence together and ask the existing schedule to synchronize. A live owner must receive a job even when no tier or starter changed. A pending import records its binding day, then becomes eligible at admission. Admission after an elapsed night owes the first copy immediately. The private cadence stays out of exports and the bucket.

The scheduler's job can disappear on a reset. Its physical firing time can reflect a backoff, and a manifest job can mean prune, sweep or expiry. Feeding that job back into every cadence decision would make the lane depend on information it cannot re-create. During a one-time repair of historical objects, a surviving unambiguous first-night appointment can preserve its day. When an object retains a generation, use its recorded start. When it retains a real last-attempt date, preserve it. A historical owner without a dated generation, attempt, or attributable first-night appointment supplies no evidence of its original binding day. Establish a new cadence on the first repaired observation in that case. A restore can have a sweep due even with no writing generation, and an adopted row can disappear on expiry. Neither event establishes a first binding.

Check the first real wake, a door across midnight before the owner's offset, an absent/rebuilt job, a missed wake, a granted owner without first binding, a first import, a previously given-up generation and a restored owner. Check the old unbound condition and the idle generation's lack of further work. The existing `Backups.#open` atomicity and the separate pass-failure backoff determine what happens when opening is interrupted. Pre-cutover verification still requires their combination.

A historical object can have a pre-wipe pin of a generation from another namespace. If the owner stopped before it opened its local undo, the database can have an owner and a pin without any local generation or cadence date. Releasing the pin on a warm object resumes its ordinary schedule immediately. Bootstrap this pre-wipe owner at wake just as an ordinary bound owner. After the wipe, the restore may have loaded only part of the owner and establishes the date when it adopts the root.

A SQL-only pending import on the initial cadence repair wrote a real sealed manifest containing its pending admission and notice. Its subsequent discard marked and emptied the primary namespace. R5r requires a live admission at the end of the generation's log. The recovered state must be admitted or suspended, including billing or abuse suspension. The automatic manifest checks that rule on appointment and dispatch. A previously due job cannot carry or sweep a pending owner's existing capture. Restore's explicit pin still carries its undo through the same backup implementation, and the export door retains its normal authority. A previous root whose historical admission is unknown remains a separate discovery concern. A current admission cannot establish the state at the end of an old log.
