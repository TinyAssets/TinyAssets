# memory-merge-settle as built (2026-11-21)

The owner memory leg's merge case failed once in the merge queue (job 136835800124, run 44900707295): its run waited for `records >= want` and never got there, `{"segments":3,"records":30981,"bytes":34806583}`. The same tree passed in the next run, with 30,988 records.

1. **The cause is the sizing record's clock, through a bound that was not a bound.** `mergeMail` derives the case's counts from one record of the mail's shape, measured on an object of its own (`own`). It widens that record into `widest` by the slack it can vary by: its three times (`TIMES_SLACK`, `Date.now() / 1000` printed with up to three decimals) and its client message id (3). It left out the seq. The measured message is its object's first (seq 1, one digit). The mail's seqs run to five digits, and two of a record's rows (the message, its recipient) carry the seq, so the mail's records are up to 8 bytes wider than any slack the bound allowed. While the sizing post's time had its three decimals (999 times in 1,000), the times' slack hid the gap. When the post landed on a whole second, its three times printed 12 characters shorter, so `own` was 1,022 against the usual 1,034. `widest` (1,037) was then under the mail's real records (about 1,041 on average), and `take`'s byte term, `(MOVE_BYTES - bytes) / widest`, predicted more of Pat's records than the merge can fit in `MOVE_BYTES`. `want` was 31,043 and the merge took 30,980, so the wait could never end.
2. **Reproduced by forcing the clock, not by load.** A scratch copy of the probe pinned `Date.now()` to a whole second for the sizing post only and ran the merge case alone in the leg's workerd. The old code then waited out its 120 s and reported `{"segments":3,"records":30981,"bytes":34806384}`, CI's figures to within 200 bytes. At a natural clock it settled (want 30,874, took 30,987). With the sizing at 12 digits (`.1`) it passed with a margin of 26 records. So the whole-second reading is the only one that fails, and it fails every time.
3. **What the brief's other candidates turned out to be.**
   - Three segments is normal. A merge's records past twice `SEGMENT_LEAST_BYTES` are cut into pieces (`pack`), and every passing run ends with three segments too.
   - The 120 s wait was not the cause. At the end the lane was idle (`fails` 0) with the merge complete, about 7 records short of the passing run because the counts had moved with `own`.
   - The client message id's slack (3) holds: the measured id is `a0000`, and the mail's run from 2 to 6 characters.
4. **The fix: count the seq in `widest`.** `SEQ_SLACK` is 2 × (5 − 1) = 8, from `MAIL_SEQ_DIGITS` (5). The counts' existing loud check now also fails if the case's own mail would reach `10 ** MAIL_SEQ_DIGITS` messages. `widest` is then at least the widest record the mail makes at any clock: at `own` 1,022 it is 1,045, against the widest of Pat's records at 1,043. So `take` is a true lower bound on the records the merge takes. `most` needs no seq term, because the measured seq is already the narrowest.
5. **`take`'s in-range term was 2 too many.** Pat's records within the seal's keys are the two after each of the muse's sealed messages but the last, so 2 × (S − 1) for a seal of S records, and S ≥ `fewest`. The term is now `2 * (fewest - 1)`. It does not bind at these figures (the byte term does), but it should still say what it means.
6. **What the case measures is unchanged.** The merge still fills `MOVE_BYTES`, so it takes the same records: 30,991 and 30,997 in the forced and natural scratch runs, against 30,987 and 30,981 before. Its peak and its passes' ends are measured at those records. Only the wait's lower bound moves: `want` is 30,766 at the natural clock against 30,874 before, 108 records lower, because Dot's and the muse's counts shift with `widest`. The bound is now one the merge always meets, so the wait is deterministic. The margins are 56 records at the whole-second sizing and 231 at the natural clock.
7. **The bodies case shares `TIMES_SLACK`, and is fine.** Its sizing post and its real posts are each their object's first two, with the same client message ids, so no seq or id slack applies. A forced whole-second sizing run of `bodies 8` still sealed one segment of two records.
