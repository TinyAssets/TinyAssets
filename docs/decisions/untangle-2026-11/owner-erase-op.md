# owner-erase-op as built (2026-11-15)

The email route's erasure (lane O/R). The site (`WebSite/site-react/app/account/page.tsx`) and the Play Data safety answers (`docs/ops/google-play-launch.md`) promise that an owner who cannot sign in can email, and the same deletion runs within 30 days. On the platform no path did it: `ownerErase` needs the owner's live session, and `OPERATOR_OPS` had no erase (the retention check's G4).

1. **One erasure, two doors that begin it.** The operator's `owner.erase` reaches the object's `erasureBegin`, a `reserved` door (the platform's caller alone), whose handler confirms its words in a unit and calls `Erasure.begin`, as `ownerErase` does. Everything after the mark is the erasure's own: its job, the erasure queue, the doors' outside step (`doors/src/erase.ts`: the directory's erased record, the keys, routes, grants and identity), the R2 delete and the wipe. No step of the erasure is written twice. The two handlers share the words check (`confirmed`); the old handler's check that a widening returned true is gone (`within` returns its act's value or throws).
2. **Three checks before anything is marked.**
   - The operator message's own gates (`OPERATOR_KEYRING`'s MAC, the window, the acceptance record), as every op's. Its acceptance record and answer are the operator log's (`OPERATOR_LOG`, locked a year).
   - The words `erase-<owner>` exactly (`eraseWords`, `contract/src/owner.ts`): the owner named a second time, after the one the MAC covers. The object holds them; the kit computes them from the same function, so no copy.
   - An owner the directory signed up (`o:` admitted): an erasure writes the erased record, which bars the name for good, so a mistyped name is refused (`refused`) and never barred. An erased one is refused by the channel's gate first.
3. **The words carry no fresh value.** `restore.wipe`'s words name the generation its pin wrote, so they are known only after a first run. An erasure has no such value: the words bind it to the owner, the signed message to the operator. The kit's mode `erase` still asks twice: a run without `--confirm` ends naming them.
4. **The kit: a mode of its own.** `erase`, with `--owner` and `--confirm`. It stops on other words and asks nothing; it plans what an erasure does (`ERASE_LOSES`). `--act` never asks `owner.erase`: `OWNER_OPS` is the `owner.` ops but one words confirm, so the confirm stays `--confirm`'s in every mode, and the op has one kit path. The workflow offers the mode.
5. **A redelivery is answered by the state it left.** Once marked, the object refuses its reserved doors `erasing` (terminal), and once the outside step wrote the directory's record the channel answers `owner_erased`. Nothing is done twice. Proved by asking again after an erasure began, and after its outside step.
