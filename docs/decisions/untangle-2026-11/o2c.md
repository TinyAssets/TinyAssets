# O2c as built (2026-11-16)

B4's contract half, second part (`o2.md` item 9; `o2b.md` "Size, and the split"; d2 B4; `tasks.md` §10, `untangle/o2c`): the door table split into `contract/doors.json`, what every caller of a door sees, and `owner/doors.json`, the owner runner's steps, each by door name.

## The plan (research, before building)

**What is there.** One table, `contract/doors.json`, 135 rows of up to 20 keys. The contract reads six of them (`contract/src/doors.ts`: the tools, their capability and arguments, the acts, the requests' generator); the owner's runner (`owner/src/owner-do.ts` `#call` and its steps) reads all of them; so a change to how the object takes a call edits the contract's file, and the contract's table holds the owner's vocabulary (`CALLER_KINDS`, `GUARDS`, `GATES`, the module a handler is in). The generator (`contract/requests.ts`, a contract tool) writes the owner's `door-map.ts` from the owner's keys.

**The split, by who reads each key.**

| Half | Keys | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `contract/doors.json` (`DoorRow`) | `door`, `class`, `capability`, `act`, `args`, `tool` | the tools and profiles (`toolTable`: a tool's name, hints, capability and arguments), the acts (`actDoors`, `policy-document.ts`), the requests and `OwnerDoors` (`requests.ts`, `owner-doors.ts`), the doors (`tools.ts` `callDoor`), the gate (`ci/gate.ts`: the names), and the runner, which reads its own row's |
| `owner/doors.json` (`DoorSteps`) | `door`, `caller`, `action`, `rule`, `receipt`, `registering`, `closed`, `live`, `guard`, `scope`, `agent`, `fieldsFirst`, `handler`, `gate`, `seeded` | the runner alone (bind, account, guard, scope, fields order, gate, seed), its typed handler map (`door-map.ts`) and the owner's guards |

- `class` is the contract's: what a suspension leaves a door is the spec's (`owner-object/spec.md`, admission), seen by every caller. `capability` is the tool's, served with it, and `act` is what a policy rule holds at the door (`policy-document.ts`). The runner reads all three from its row's contract half; none is copied.
- `caller` is the owner's (d2 B4): which of 17 account steps binds the call. Whether a door takes a binding at all is the contract's already (`UNBOUND_DOORS`, which types `OwnerDoors`), and B5's binding kinds are contract types the object picks by `caller`, so B5 adds no key here.
- `scope`, `agent` and `seeded` came after d2 (O1, slice R): runner steps, so the owner's.
- d2 listed the contract's keys as "name, class, capability, args and tool"; `act` came later (R5) and is read by the contract's policy check, so it stays.

**The join, and the rules across halves.** `owner/src/door-steps.ts` reads `owner/doors.json` and holds it to the contract's table: `stepProblems(steps, rows)` refuses a step of no door, a door with no step, a door stepped twice, an unknown key, caller, guard, scope, gate or handler name, and the rules that tie a step to its contract row: no binding exactly on `UNBOUND_DOORS`; `admission` exactly on the reserved and erasure callers; `import` exactly on the import and discard callers; a seeded door is a `units` door; an owner's door names its action and a session's its rule. `ownerRows()` is each contract row with its steps, in the contract's order, held whole by both halves' problems, and `ownerRow(name)` one of them. The contract's `doorProblems` keeps its own keys' rules, and `class` null exactly on `UNBOUND_DOORS` in place of `caller` `none`. The object's static block holds `ownerRows()` before it defines a method, so a row in one half with no match in the other fails the class's load, and its guard.

**The generator.** `door-map.ts` is the owner's (its `DoorRows` are the module, caller and gate of each door), so it is written by an owner tool, `owner/write-door-map.ts`, from `ownerRows()`; `contract/requests.ts` keeps `requests.ts` and `owner-doors.ts`, from the contract half alone, and no longer writes into `owner/`. A contract tool cannot import the owner (`ci/kit/layers.ts`, "a workspace's tools"), so this is the only shape that reads both. The two generators share how they write TypeScript (the width, a doc comment wrapped to it, a type broken at it): `contract/src/source-lines.ts`, the one module both tools and the ci kit's `types.ts` may import, whose `WIDTH` copy goes. `npm run generate` runs the owner's tool after the contract's. A step's door missing from `owner/doors.json` is then refused three ways: the join throws in the generator, the generated `DoorRows` lacks the door so `DoorMethods`' `RequestIn<H, "door">` fails `tsc`, and the owner's guard.

**The readers, and the half each reads.**
- Contract only, unchanged: `doors/src/{tools,mcp,app}.ts`, `owner/src/codemode.ts`, `contract/src/policy-document.ts`, `ci/gate.ts`, `owner/test/record/{rv,rpc}.record.ts`, `owner/test/{owner,erasure,agent-mail}.test.ts`, and every `doors/test/` and `doors/guards/` reader (tools and profiles). Lane T's `untangle/t2c` touches `doors/test/`; this PR does not.
- Both halves, moved to `ownerRows()`/`ownerRow()`: the runner (`owner-do.ts`), `owner/guards/{write-gate,admission-caller,policy-boundary}.test.ts`.
- The owner's types: `owner/src/door.ts` takes `CallerKind` from `door-steps.ts`.
- Guards: `contract/guards/doors.test.ts` keeps its keys' cases; the step cases (caller, gate, guard, seed, handler, action, rule, class by caller) move to a new `owner/guards/doors.test.ts`, with the join's (a step with no door, a door with no step, one twice). `contract/guards/tools.test.ts` holds each tool's hints by its own capability (`tool.capability`, which is its row's); its check that each tool's door is outside-callable, and each outside-callable door has a tool or is owed (`DEFERRED`, `DOORS_ONLY`), reads `caller`, so it moves to the owner's guard, beside the join.
- Comments and data that name where a row's step is: each module's "THE DOORS: the `x` rows' handlers" line, `door.ts`, `owner-do.ts`, `write-gate.test.ts`, and the cutover manifest (the eight import steps in `owner/doors.json`, the import and discard callers in `door-steps.ts`).

**Size.** The JSON is about 300 lines moved (the steps out of 135 contract rows, the same in the owner's file), the contract module and generator about 250 changed, the owner module about 200 new, the runner and readers about 100, the guards about 300 moved: about 1,200 lines, one PR.

**Departures from d2 B4.** The owner's table is a list of rows each naming its door, not an object keyed by door: a JSON object's duplicate key is lost at parse with no error, and a list's is refused. The door map's generator is the owner's, not the contract's (above).

## As built

1. **`contract/doors.json`** holds 135 rows of `door`, `class`, `capability`, `act`, `args` and `tool`, each row's head re-packed by a script that copied every `args` and `tool` block as it was and checked each row's two halves together equal to the old row. `DoorRow` is those six keys, and `doorProblems` their rules: a key of the steps in a contract row is refused (`a key gate`), and `class` is null exactly on `UNBOUND_DOORS`.
2. **`owner/doors.json`** holds the 135 doors' steps, one row each, in the contract's order. **`owner/src/door-steps.ts`** has the steps' vocabulary (`CALLER_KINDS`, `GUARDS`, `GATES`, moved from the contract), `DoorSteps`, `OwnerRow` (the two halves of a door), `stepProblems`, `ownerRows()` and `ownerRow(name)`. The runner, its static block and its types read `OwnerRow`; the static block holds `ownerRows()`, so a door of either half with no match in the other fails the class's load.
3. **The generators.** `contract/requests.ts` writes `requests.ts` and `owner-doors.ts`, unchanged; `owner/write-door-map.ts` writes `door-map.ts`, whose only change is its header. `npm run generate` runs both. `contract/src/source-lines.ts` holds `WIDTH` and the doc comment's wrapping (`docLines`) for both and for `ci/kit/types.ts`, whose own `WIDTH` went. The contract's `frozen` and the `one` test the two halves both use are `checks.ts`'s (`frozen`, `among`).
4. **The guards.** The step cases left `contract/guards/doors.test.ts` for the new `owner/guards/doors.test.ts`, with the join's (each refused with its words), the class held against the steps (the import's and the discard's doors of another class among them), and the outside-callable doors' check from `contract/guards/tools.test.ts`, which now holds each tool's hints by its own `capability`. `admission-caller.test.ts` loses its two equalities of class and caller, which are `stepProblems`' rules word for word; its pins of the import doors by name stay.
5. **The cutover manifest** names the eight import steps in `owner/doors.json`, the import and discard callers in `door-steps.ts` and their cases in the owner's guard; the contract's guard has no import case left, so its entry goes.
6. **Size:** 1,842 changed lines, past the plan's 1,200: the plan counted the moved JSON once, and git counts its 694 lines on both sides. Without the two tables it is 1,148. Nothing in it lands alone: the tables, their readers and the generator move together, or the tree does not build.
7. **Left:** `caller` `none` and `UNBOUND_DOORS` say one fact in two places, held equal by `stepProblems`, as before this PR. B5's binding kinds (O3, `untangle/o3`, owed by `tasks.md`'s O2c line) make the binding a contract type the object picks by its caller, which removes the second.
